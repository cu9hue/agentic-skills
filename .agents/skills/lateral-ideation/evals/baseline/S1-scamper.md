First I break Granola into parts, so each SCAMPER move has something specific to act on:

- **Input:** audio from a video call on your laptop. No bot joins.
- **User:** a knowledge worker (founder, investor, PM) who runs many meetings.
- **Human role:** you type rough notes, and the AI fills them in from the transcript.
- **Output:** a cleaned-up notes doc.
- **Timing:** after the meeting.
- **Unit:** one meeting at a time.
- **Model:** per-seat SaaS, sold bottom-up.

A tweak changes one of these parts and keeps the same user and buyer. A new direction changes the user, the buyer, or the job. I kept only moves that do one of those.

---

## S: Substitute

**1. Replace the video call with an in-person site visit.** A contractor walks a house with the homeowner. The app turns that walk into a scope of work and a draft quote. Trades lose hours every week to writing quotes at night.
- *Buyer:* small trade businesses (roofing, HVAC, remodeling).
- *Risk:* noisy job sites. The money is in the quote, not the notes, so you have to get pricing right.

**2. Replace the professional with the other side of the table.** Doctors already have scribes (Abridge and others). Patients and family caregivers have nothing. The app records the visit for the patient, explains it in plain language, tracks follow-ups, and shares it with a sibling who couldn't attend.
- *Buyer:* adult children who manage an aging parent's care, or an employer benefit.
- *Risk:* consent rules on recording, and consumers' willingness to pay.

## C: Combine

**3. Combine every customer call across a company into one research base.** Product teams want to know "what did customers say about pricing this quarter?" Sales and CS calls already hold the answer, but they sit in Gong and nobody on the product side reads them. The product is voice-of-customer research built from calls the company already records.
- *Buyer:* product and research leads.
- *Risk:* Gong, Dovetail, and the note-takers can all move here. You need a sharper wedge than "search your calls."

**4. Combine notes with the system of record, so the CRM fills itself.** I mention this because it is the obvious one. Attio, Day.ai, and most note-takers are already here. Skip it unless you have a specific vertical.

## A: Adapt

**5. Adapt game-film review from sports to managers.** Gong coaches sales reps on their calls. Nobody does this for a new manager's 1:1s, skip-levels, and hard conversations. The product reviews your meetings and coaches you on them: talk ratio, avoided topics, unclear commitments.
- *Buyer:* L&D or People teams, which already pay for leadership coaching.
- *Risk:* employees may not accept their 1:1s being recorded. Trust is the whole product.

**6. Adapt compliance record-keeping to other regulated conversations.** Financial advisors already have tools for this (Jump, Zocks). Look at the next regulated conversation down the list: insurance brokers, mortgage officers, HR investigations. Each one has a regulator who demands a record.
- *Buyer:* compliance, not the user. That means a bigger budget and a slower sale.

## M: Modify (magnify / minify)

**7. Magnify the time horizon into a company decision log.** One meeting's notes have little value. Three years of "why did we decide X, who was in the room, what did we reject" is the institutional memory every company loses when people leave.
- *Buyer:* the COO or chief of staff at a 200 to 2,000 person company.
- *Risk:* the value shows up late. You have to sell it before it exists.

**8. Magnify the room into public meetings.** City councils, zoning boards, and utility commissions hold thousands of recorded meetings that nobody reads. Developers, lobbyists, and local reporters pay to know when their topic comes up.
- *Buyer:* real estate developers, public affairs firms, newsrooms.
- *Risk:* a niche market, and some players already exist. The data is public, so speed and coverage are the moat.

## P: Put to other uses

**9. Use the transcript as knowledge transfer, not memory.** A senior engineer at a utility or manufacturer retires next year. The app runs structured interviews with them, records them walking through the plant, and turns that into searchable know-how for whoever replaces them.
- *Buyer:* operations leaders in industries with an aging workforce.
- *Risk:* services-heavy at first. It might be a consultancy that wears a product costume.

**10. Use the record as evidence.** Landlords, contractors, and HR teams get into disputes over "you said X." The product is a neutral, timestamped record that both parties agree to up front.
- *Risk:* people avoid recording the conversations that later turn into disputes.

## E: Eliminate

**11. Eliminate the notes, keep only the actions.** No doc at all. After the meeting the tickets exist, the follow-up email sits in drafts, and the calendar invite has gone out. This edges toward a tweak, but it is a different product if you sell it as "meeting to done" to ops-heavy teams.

**12. Eliminate the meeting.** Standups and status meetings exist to collect updates. An agent interviews each person for three minutes on their own schedule, then sends one synthesis to the manager. The product competes with the meeting, not with the note-taker.
- *Buyer:* engineering and ops managers.
- *Risk:* status meetings also serve a social purpose, and this kills that part too.

## R: Reverse / Rearrange

**13. Reverse the timing: before, not after.** The product is the prep, not the notes. "Last time you spoke with Dana she pushed back on price, promised an intro to her CFO, and mentioned her kid's surgery. Here are three things to ask." This is strongest for people whose job is relationships: investors, account executives, recruiters.

**14. Reverse who asks the questions.** The AI runs the interview instead of recording it. User research at scale, where 200 customer interviews run overnight. Listen Labs and Outset are already here, so this proves the direction, but it is crowded.

**15. Reverse from capture to rehearsal.** Practice the hard conversation before it happens, against a simulation built from real past calls. Sales roleplay exists (Hyperbound). Rehearsal for managers, doctors delivering bad news, or negotiators is open.

---

## Where I'd look first

| Direction | Why it's worth a look | Biggest unknown |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Site visit to quote (trades)** | Clear pain, clear ROI, underserved buyer, and the output is money, not notes | Whether quote accuracy is reachable |
| **2. Patient-side visit recorder** | Large emotional pain, and incumbents serve the other side | Who pays |
| **7. Company decision log** | Defensible: the data compounds and is hard to rebuild | Whether anyone buys a value that shows up in year two |

I would deprioritize 4, 11, and 14. Well-funded teams are already there, and Granola itself can ship 4 and 11 as features.

Two questions would sharpen this. What is your edge: a domain you know, a buyer you can reach, or a technical angle? And do you want B2B only, or is consumer in play? Those two answers would cut this list to two or three directions worth testing with real conversations this week.
