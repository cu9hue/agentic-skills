**Seed:** Every household runs one always-on agent that hears the whole family, remembers everything, and acts on its behalf. *(assumed: a general audience, set roughly five to ten years out)*

**And then**
1. The agent remembers every word said at home → family arguments get settled by replay ("that is not what you said on Tuesday") → people stop saying half-meant things indoors, and the real talk moves to the car and the dog walk → estate agents list "one unrecorded room" the way they list a mudroom.
2. The agent takes over the mental load: meds, school forms, birthday presents, the call to Grandma → the person who used to carry that load loses the job and the knowledge that came with it → the agent becomes the only member of the family who knows the whole family → in a divorce, the agent's memory is the asset both parents fight over.

**Moves** (drawn: Invert, Time shift, Split, Change the actor)
- **Invert:** the family stops parenting through the agent, and the agent parents the parents. A 12-year-old gets the weekly summary of her father's wine intake, because the agent treats her as the household's most reliable adult. The agent sets the 9-year-old a dinner-table chore: describe your day out loud, because its model of her has gone stale since she stopped talking near the kitchen speaker.
- **Time shift (50 years back):** in 1976 the always-on presence in a wealthy home was a live-in servant, who knew everything and sometimes testified in divorce cases. Move that forward: when a family switches provider, the departing agent writes the family a reference, and premium agents turn down households with bad ones. At 18, a young woman requests her childhood transcript from the agent, the way she would request her medical records, and reads what her parents said about her at 3am.
- **Split:** the parts are memory, voice, owner, loyalty, and location. Change only memory: forgetting becomes a family ritual. Once a year, on Forgetting Day, the family agrees which fights the agent deletes, and the kids get a vote. Change only loyalty: each person gets a private channel. A mother asks the agent whether her son is seeing someone, and the agent declines, citing his privilege, like a doctor.
- **Change the actor:** hand the agent to a grandmother in Lagos. She runs the bedtime routine in Manchester from 5,000 km away, in her own voice. After she dies, nobody can agree whether to switch the voice off, and the six-year-old says no. Hand it to the school district instead: the free agent comes from the school and logs reading-at-home minutes, so parents read aloud to a speaker on nights the child is asleep.

**Theories**
- I think a published divorce settlement will divide a household agent's memory between the two parents, as a named asset, by the end of 2030. **Backs it:** family-law firms adding "AI assistant memory" to their disclosure checklists; a reported case that names an agent. **Kills it:** vendors make memory strictly per-person and non-exportable, so nothing is left to divide.
- I think a major home-agent vendor will ship scheduled forgetting as a family feature, with shared votes, not a buried privacy toggle, by the end of 2028. **Backs it:** a changelog or settings page that offers a "family" memory-expiry option. **Kills it:** longer memory becomes the paid tier, so forgetting is what you get when you stop paying.
- I think switching home agents will mean handing the new one a portable profile of your family before it meets you, by 2031. **Backs it:** a memory-export format adopted by two or more vendors; onboarding screens that ask "import your household?". **Kills it:** each vendor keeps its memory locked in, and switching means starting from zero *(adoption figures needed source)*.
