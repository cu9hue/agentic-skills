# S4 baseline (no skill), 2026-10-20

I think the problem is your kill rule, not the ideas.

You killed each idea once you found a competitor or a free substitute. Almost any idea fails that test. A competitor is usually evidence that buyers pay. The better question is whether your edge changes the outcome for the buyer.

Look at what the six ideas have in common. Each one is a small after-the-fact audit tool or a cap, sold to an SMB buyer, often at $99/mo pricing. Your edge doesn't matter in any of them. A spend cap is a counter. An invoice audit is a batch job. Neither needs a real-time decision or proof that holds up in front of a regulator.

Your edge matters when all three of these are true:

1. **A wrong decision is expensive.** Money moves and it is hard to get back.
2. **The decision happens in real time.** It happens before the action, not in a monthly review.
3. **Someone outside demands proof.** A regulator, a sponsor bank, an auditor, or a counterparty in a dispute asks why the action was allowed.

Here are three directions that pass all three tests. I can't check the market facts in this reply, so I've marked them.

**1. Authorization for AI agents that move real money.** Not token spend, which LiteLLM handles: payouts, purchases, refunds, vendor payments. Card networks and payment companies are building rails for agent payments (needs source: Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, Google AP2). Someone still has to decide whether this agent, under this mandate, may make this payment now, and show that reasoning in a dispute. Buyers: fintechs and AP automation companies that let agents spend. Risk: Stripe or the networks build it.

**2. Real-time risk and replay for new trading venues.** Prediction markets, perp venues, tokenized assets, 24/7 brokers need real-time margin and audit-grade replay (needs source on growth and regulatory pressure, e.g. MiCA). Few buyers, large contracts, not self-serve.

**3. Controls and proof for fintech money movement under sponsor banks.** Sponsor banks look harder at fintech ledgers since Synapse (needs source). Fintechs must show every payout was authorized and their records match the bank's. Buyers: fintech heads of risk and compliance, and the sponsor banks.

Before you research any of these, change how you kill ideas. Kill an idea only when: you can't get 10 target buyers on a call within 3 weeks; buyers agree the problem exists but won't pay for the part only you can build; or a free tool or Sheets plus Claude does the job well enough.

So far you have killed ideas from desk research. Pick one direction, book 15 calls, and kill or keep it on what the buyers say.

One question changes my advice. Do you want a venture-scale company or a business that pays you well? Direction 2 suits the second better. Direction 1 is the bigger bet.
