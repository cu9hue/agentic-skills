# Socratic question bank

Six categories of Socratic questions, from UConn CETL, "Socratic Questions"
(https://cetl.uconn.edu/resources/teaching-your-course/leading-effective-discussions/socratic-questions/).
Stems are verbatim; adapt them to the material at hand.

## 1. Clarification

Seek understanding and focus the discussion.

- Why do you say that?
- What do you mean by…?
- How does this relate to our discussion?
- What do you think is the main issue?
- Could you expand upon that point further?

## 2. Probing assumptions

Examine the premises underneath a claim.

- Why would someone make this assumption?
- What could we assume instead?
- How can you verify or disprove that assumption?

## 3. Probing reasons and evidence

Investigate how a conclusion was reached.

- What would be an example?
- What do you think causes … to happen?
- Why?
- What other information do we need?
- By what reasoning did you come to that conclusion?
- Is there reason to doubt that evidence?

## 4. Implications and consequences

Explore where a claim leads.

- What generalizations can you make?
- What are the consequences of that assumption?
- What are you implying?
- How does … affect …?
- How does … tie in with what we learned before?

## 5. Viewpoints and perspectives

Consider alternative interpretations.

- What would be an alternative?
- What is another way to look at it?
- How would other groups of people respond, and why?
- What might someone who believed … think?
- Would you explain why it is necessary or beneficial?
- What are the strengths and weaknesses of …?
- How are … and … similar?
- What is a counterargument for …?

## 6. Questioning the question

Reflect on the inquiry itself.

- Why is this question important?
- What was the point of this question?
- Why do you think I asked this question?
- What does … mean?
- How does … apply to everyday life?
