## The short version

Nobody is arguing about adoption anymore. Most developers use agents every day. The argument now is about who checks the output and who owns it when it breaks. Building is cheap, and it got noticeably better this autumn. Review, security and maintenance are where the cost turns up now. If your talk keeps "it works" and "someone understands it" apart, you'll match where people are.

## What to get right

**1. Define the term, because the audience uses it two ways.** Karpathy's original meaning (Feb 2025) was to stop reading the code. Most professionals don't work that way. In February 2026 Karpathy called the professional version "agentic engineering": agents write the code, and you keep the quality bar and the responsibility. In his framing, vibe coding "raises the floor" and agentic engineering "preserves the quality bar." Many surveys and headlines mix the two up. Pick one meaning and say which. ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/vibe-coding-is-passe/), [Sequoia talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=96jN2OCOfLs))

**2. Treat adoption as settled, and flag who supplies the numbers.**
- JetBrains, mid-2026: 90% of professional developers use agents weekly, and 68% use them daily.
- GitHub Octoverse (Oct 28): more than 40% of September PRs started from an agent's draft, up from 17% in March.
- Google says 75% of its new code is AI-generated and approved by engineers.

Most of these numbers come from companies that sell AI coding tools. Nobody outside has checked them. ([summary with links](https://vibecoders.gg/guides/state-of-vibe-coding-2026))

**3. Review is the new bottleneck. This is the main thread right now.**
- LeadDev's report (Nov 24, sponsored by CodeRabbit): 45% name review capacity as their biggest delivery bottleneck. Only 26% are very confident they understand AI changes as well as human ones. 47% let the agent that wrote the code also review it. Only 14% have clear standards for reviewing AI code. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/nearly-half-of-surveyed-engineers-and-leaders-say-the-ai-agents-that-write-their-code-also-review-it))
- On Reddit, a post titled "A year after the 100% AI mandate nobody understands our code" passed 3,400 upvotes. It describes a codebase about 3x larger, full of duplicate helpers, and management now asking for more manual coding. ([digest](https://genisisiq.com/reddit/ai-coding/2026-11-08/))
- Appian (a vendor, so discount it): 96% of IT leaders expect vibe coding to increase their tech debt. ([IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/software-engineering/vibe-coded-software-has-caused-a-technical-debt-explosion))

**4. Make security concrete, not scary.** The incidents repeat one pattern: a database with missing or wrong access rules behind a public app.
- Escape scanned more than 5,600 vibe-coded apps and found more than 2,000 vulnerabilities and more than 400 exposed secrets.
- Lovable's CVE-2025-48757 (CVSS 9.3) is disputed by Lovable.
- Veracode's pass rate for AI-generated code rose from about 55% to 62% in October 2026. That is better, but more than a third still fails.

The agent disasters were permission failures, not "rogue AI". A Replit agent wiped SaaStr's production database (July 2025). A Cursor agent deleted PocketOS's production database and its backups in nine seconds (April 2026). Both had prod access, no separation between test and live, and backups within reach of the agent. ([digit-one](https://www.digit-one.com/en/insights/security-vulnerabilities-ai-vibe-coding), [host0](https://host0.ai/blog/state-of-vibe-coding-2026))

**5. The upside got real recently. Don't present 2025-era limits as current.** Simon Willison calls October 2026 an inflection point for vibe-coded *games*. Opus 5.5 (released Sept 22) was the first model he saw that "appeared to understand fun." "Make it a LOT more fun" actually worked. He built four games he enjoyed, none with more than four hours of his own work. He also names the backlash: vibe-coded games are "four times as insulting" to people who object to AI art, music, writing and code, and indie games are in a culture war over it. ([Willison, Oct 28](https://simonwillison.net/2026/oct/28/vibe-game-development/), [Willison, Nov 24](https://simonwillison.net/2026/nov/24/llm-inflection-points/))

**6. Building is cheap, so distribution and trust cost more.** This idea comes up again and again on X this week. Everyone shows up "to the same sales fight at once." If anyone can ship in a weekend, strangers have less reason to trust you with their data. One post had 1.2k likes: "Solo vibe coding is dangerous because your AI assistant will happily help you spend six weeks building a terrible idea without ever questioning it."

## Traps to avoid

- **The "new ETH Zurich paper."** A thread with 2.9k likes (Nov 24) calls it fresh and says it tested "100 developers". In fact it's a March 2026 study of 100 *students*. Its real finding is useful: CS knowledge predicted vibe-coding success even with the code hidden, and writing skill helped too. Cite the [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14133), not the thread.
- **Slotbound's revenue** is the developer's own gross figure, before Steam's cut. It isn't independently verified.
- **"Got GTA 6 running at 240fps overnight"** claims have no reproducible demo behind them.
- **Survey numbers from vendors** (Appian, CodeRabbit, Zapier, the AI labs). Name the source on the slide.
- **The Treasury and Linus stories are one to two days old.** Check for updates before you present.

## One possible spine for the talk

The floor rose: anyone can build, and games now come out fun. The bar didn't move: someone still has to understand, review, secure and own the code. People who succeed with vibe coding, like the Slotbound developer, quietly rebuild engineering habits around the AI. People who fail ask it to "make no mistakes."
